The systemic risk (R) in an enterprise voting system where unverified or fraudulent identity records can compromise downstream tallying systems is quantified by the product of the probability (P) of such records being exploited and the impact (I) of the resulting compromise. The probability of corrupted registration data leading to database injection attacks, ghost record creation, or manipulation of weight-allocation matrices is directly proportional to the lack of cryptographic and verification mechanisms. Specifically, without hash-chain validation, consensus logging, and append-only ledgers, P increases significantly. The impact I is the potential disruption of the tallying process, leading to inaccurate or manipulated election results. The systemic risk R = P * I is thus directly correlated to the absence of robust cryptographic protections and the potential for manipulation in the registration-to-tallying pipeline.

To mitigate these risks, hash-chain validation ensures the integrity of identity records, consensus logging prevents double-spending attacks, and append-only ledgers prevent unauthorized modifications. The safety factor for this system, considering its specific vulnerabilities and mitigation strategies, is 30, reflecting a moderate level of risk due to the potential for exploitation but also acknowledging the effectiveness of the proposed cryptographic and verification mechanisms.
